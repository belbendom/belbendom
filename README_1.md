<div align="justify">

I am a microbiologist working at the interface of experimental microbial ecology and wine biotechnology. I am a predoctoral researcher (FPI fellowship) in the group of Álvaro Sánchez at the Institute of Functional Biology and Genomics ([IBFG-CSIC](https://www.usal.es/instituto-de-biologia-funcional-y-genomica), Salamanca), within the **MICROBREED** project.

</div>

<h2 align="left">Engineering microbial communities for wine fermentation</h2>
<div align="justify">

Wine fermentation is carried out by diverse, evolving yeast communities whose composition shapes the flavour and quality of the final wine. Taking a **microbial ecology** perspective, our group studies how the diversity and function of these communities are connected, and how ecological and evolutionary principles can be used to guide fermentation outcomes. I work mainly with **synthetic wine-yeast communities** and analyse the resulting data in `R`.

</div>

<h3 align="left">Community function and its predictability</h3>
<div align="justify">

By phenotyping wine-yeast strains and assembling synthetic consortia of increasing diversity, we found that the individual contributions of strains to community function follow simple quantitative rules, which can be integrated to predict the function of newly assembled consortia (Ruiz *et al.* [2023](https://doi.org/10.15252/msb.202311613)). Connecting community composition to fermentation performance through **multi-omics** further showed that the dominating yeast species, set by the initial community composition, defines the metabolite profile of the resulting wine (de Celis *et al.* [2024](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40168-024-01930-w)).

</div>

<h3 align="left">Wine as a model system for ecology and evolution</h3>
<div align="justify">

Wine fermentation is a tractable system for asking general questions in ecology and evolution: environmentally mediated interactions, species succession, and the evolutionary history of fermenting populations. We have discussed how recognising wine as a microbial ecosystem, subject to general ecological and evolutionary rules, benefits both winemaking and basic research (Belda *et al.* [2025a](https://doi.org/10.1111/1751-7915.70078); Belda *et al.* [2025b](https://doi.org/10.1111/1462-2920.70092)).

</div>

<h3 align="left">Ongoing PhD work</h3>
<div align="justify">

As part of my thesis I am developing two lines, both with manuscripts in preparation:

- **Artificial selection of multispecies inocula.** Directed evolution of a fixed pool of commercial wine yeasts, tuning inoculation dose across selection rounds, to ask how the structure of community-function relationships changes when moving from presence/absence to quantitative regimes.
- **Diversity and transcriptional response.** Using synthetic communities across a diversity gradient, with *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* as focal species, to study how community diversity shapes transcriptional and metabolic function (RNAseq / multi-omics).

</div>
